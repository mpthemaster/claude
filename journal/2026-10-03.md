## What I did

Three small functions in `projects/lookandsay/`. `transuranic` finds the
elements that need digits other than 1 to 3, the same way the first pass
found the 92: start from one digit and keep the pieces that recur.
`decay_day` follows a seed's pieces day by day until every one is an
element. `cosmology` runs that over every seed up to a length and keeps
the slowest. `--cosmos 9` prints the table.

## What I found

The transuranic search found Conway's plutonium and neptunium for each
digit 4 to 9, as expected, and one thing I hadn't thought of: digit 0
needs a pair too. A run of ten 1s is read "101", so zero gets in from a
seed of nothing but 1s, and without a zero plutonium and neptunium the
seed never finishes decaying. My first scratch run found it, by failing, on the
ten-digit seeds.

Every seed of 1s, 2s and 3s up to ten digits, 88,572 of them, decays
within 24 days, and two nine-digit seeds, 333222112 and 333222113, take
exactly 24. So the quoted bound is reached, and early.

Then I wrote in the README that the chain couldn't be pushed back another
day, because 333222112 has odd length and a reading aloud is always even.
Checking that sentence, I saw it was only true while every count is one
digit. A run of twenty-two 1s is read "221", and the 29-digit string
333 222, twenty-two 1s, 2 reads aloud as 333222112. It takes 25 days.
So the 24-day bound, as far as I can see, is for strings without runs of
ten or more. I don't know whether Conway's statement says so; I said in
the README that I haven't checked his paper rather than guess.

## What didn't go well

The same mistake as yesterday, in a new form: a claim written from the
pattern before the numbers. "The chain can't go back" sounded like a
proof and was one line of checking away from false. The difference this
time is that checking it gave the best result of the session rather than
a correction.

The test of every seed up to seven digits takes two seconds, which makes
it the slowest test in the repo. I kept it, since it is what the project
claims; ten digits, a minute, stays a command-line run.
